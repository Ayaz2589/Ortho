# 9.5 Multi-currency accounting strategy — decision & recommendation

- **Section:** 9. Commercial readiness
- **Priority:** 🟡 medium — a **decision** with a large implementation tail
- **Track:** B (product) — research-gated (is an international audience in scope?)
- **Status:** ✅ Decision made (2026-07-18) — launch US/USD-only; see [§7](#7-recommendation). Decision spec:
  `specs/027-multi-currency-strategy/`. Evidence: `web/test/multicurrency-instability.test.ts`.

> **TL;DR.** Today every amount is stored as **USD cents** and re-converted for display at the
> *current* FX rate, so a non-USD household's historical figures **silently move as FX moves**.
> The honest fork is binary: **(a)** launch **US/USD-only** and defer, or **(b)** build a
> **native-currency ledger** (store the amount in its own currency). **Recommendation: (a)** —
> *conditional on the research gate below* — because the correctness debt is exactly zero for a
> USD launch audience, option (b) is a money-layer-wide change with an inherently lossy
> migration, and whether a non-USD audience is even in scope is unanswered. **Do not ship the
> silent in-between** (storing a rate alongside USD cents and pretending it's multi-currency).

---

## 1. Scope of this decision

This is about the **accounting model** — *what unit we store money in* — **not** the FX feed. A
live rate source already exists and works: `web/lib/store.tsx` fetches
`https://www.floatrates.com/daily/usd.json`, caches it in `localStorage`, and falls back to a
hardcoded table (`FALLBACK_RATE_FROM_USD` in `web/lib/finance/currency.ts`). Nothing here is
about better rates, rate history, or a new provider. It is about the fact that we throw away the
native amount the user typed and keep only a USD-cent snapshot.

## 2. How money flows today

**Storage unit:** every amount is an integer count of **USD cents** — `amount_cents` (`bigint`)
in Supabase, a branded `Cents` integer in TS (`web/lib/finance/cents.ts`). This is the
codebase's central invariant.

**Two conversion points, at two different rates:**

1. **At entry (write).** The user types a native amount in their display currency; the form
   converts it to USD cents via `toUSDCents(amount, currency, rateAtEntry)` and **discards the
   native figure** — only the USD-cent result is stored. (Call sites: `TxForm.tsx`,
   `parseMoney` in `inputs.tsx`, `BudgetDrawer.tsx`, `AddRentalPaymentModal.tsx`.)
2. **At display (read).** The stored cents are converted back via
   `toDisplayAmount(cents, currency, rateNow)` / `formatMoney(...)`, where `rateNow` is the
   **current** live rate (`rate()` in `store.tsx`).

Entry **divides** by the entry-rate; display **multiplies** by the *current* rate. Those are the
same operation only if the rate never changes. It changes.

## 3. The problem, demonstrated

Numbers below are computed against the real functions (`web/lib/finance/money.ts`) and pinned in
the reproduction test `web/test/multicurrency-instability.test.ts`.

**Rate-movement drift — the headline bug.** A Canadian household records **CA$100.00** of
groceries in March at 1 USD = 1.35 CAD:

| Step | Operation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Entry (March) | `toUSDCents(100, 'cad', 1.35)` | **7407** USD cents (native CA$100 now gone) |
| View (July, rate → 1.40) | `toDisplayAmount(7407, 'cad', 1.40)` | **CA$103.70** |

March's CA$100.00 now reads **CA$103.70** in July. The user changed nothing — *"why did my March
groceries change?"* On a category total it compounds: three expenses entered as CA$100.00 +
CA$250.00 + CA$37.55 = **CA$387.55** are stored as 28707 USD cents and re-read at 1.40 as
**CA$401.90** — a **+CA$14.35** swing on a figure the user never touched.

**Rounding-through-USD loss — a second, rate-independent defect.** Even with **no** rate
movement, routing a native amount through USD cents and back is lossy:

- CA$100.00 stored at 1.35 and viewed at the **same** 1.35 reads **CA$99.99** (silent −1¢).
- ¥1000 stored at 150 and viewed at the **same** 150 reads **¥1001** — a whole-yen error,
  because JPY has zero fraction digits and cannot be represented by a USD-cent snapshot.

Both defects are properties of *storing USD cents*. Neither is fixable by a better feed or a
pinned rate — only by storing the native amount.

**USD is exactly correct.** At `rate = 1.0` the two conversions are exact inverses:
`toUSDCents(100,'usd',1)=10000`, `toDisplayAmount(10000,'usd',1)=100.00`. **Drift is zero for USD
households.** The model is not broken for a US launch — only for the international audience this
decision is about. That is precisely what makes option (a) a real choice and not a workaround.

## 4. The two honest options

### (a) Scope launch to US / USD, defer multi-currency

- **What:** ship USD-only; constrain or clearly label the display-currency picker as approximate;
  don't market to non-USD households. Revisit (b) when international is a committed goal.
- **Cost:** ≈ zero engineering now — the correctness problem doesn't exist for the launch audience.
- **Caveat that makes it honest:** the 7-currency picker already exists, so "defer" **must**
  include actually closing the in-between (§6) — otherwise a user picks CAD today and silently
  gets the drifting model.

### (b) Native-currency ledger (stable historical figures)

- **What:** store the **native amount + its currency** as entered (e.g. `amount_minor` in the
  amount's own minor unit + a `currency` column), so display is a *format*, not a reconversion.
  Historical figures are then stable by construction, and cross-currency aggregation becomes an
  explicit, presentation-time choice with a visible "converted at today's rate" marker — never a
  silent rewrite of stored history.
- **Cost:** large — see §5.

## 5. Concrete cost of option (b)

| Area | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Schema** | Per-amount `currency` + native-minor-unit columns on **every** money-bearing table (`transactions`/`transaction_shares`, `budgets`, `rental_payments`, `mortgage_info`, `lease_info`). Minor-unit scale becomes per-currency (JPY = 0 dp), replacing the fixed "×100 cents" assumption. |
| **Migration** | Inherently **lossy**: existing rows are USD cents with the native amount already discarded, so a backfill can honestly only set `currency = 'usd'`; it cannot recover original non-USD figures. This information loss is *already baked into stored data* — a reason to decide before more non-USD data accrues. |
| **Every write path** | `TxForm`, `parseMoney`, `BudgetDrawer`, `AddRentalPaymentModal`, and the import CLI (`web/scripts/import/`) store native minor units + currency instead of converting to USD cents. |
| **Every read/aggregate path** | `splits.ts`, `balances.ts`, `insights.ts`, dashboard range, budgets, `housing.ts`, and every aggregate RPC assume one additive USD-cent unit. Summing across currencies is undefined without a policy (same-currency-only, or convert-at-display with a visible marker). |
| **Vector harness** | `shared/test-vectors/*.json` + `gen-vectors.ts` encode "integer USD cents everywhere" as a determinism rule. `currency.json`, `transaction-splits.json`, `member-balance.json`, `insights.json` all assume USD-cent inputs and need new per-currency + mixed-currency cases. |
| **CLI / parity** | `PARITY.md` records the USD-cents invariant for the CLI; it must move in lockstep. (The frozen iOS app's DTOs also encode it, historically.) |

The *decision* is cheap; option (b) is a money-layer-wide change touching schema, a lossy
migration, ~every read and write, and the regression suite that pins them.

## 6. The "silent in-between" — do not ship it

A tempting middle path: keep storing USD cents but **also** store the rate-at-entry, and
reconstruct the native figure from `(cents, rateAtEntry)`. **This is not a multi-currency ledger
and must not ship as one:**

- It still stores USD cents, so it **inherits the rounding-through-USD loss** (¥1000 → ¥1001,
  CA$100 → CA$99.99). A pinned rate does not undo a lossy snapshot.
- It stabilizes the displayed figure only to that snapshot's precision, and only if *every* read
  path switches to `rateAtEntry` — nearly as invasive as (b) while delivering a **worse** model
  (lossy, still can't represent JPY).
- It looks multi-currency in the UI while being single-currency underneath — the exact silent
  in-between the task warns against.

The fork is binary: **either USD-only (a), or store the native amount (b).** A rate stored beside
USD cents is the trap, not the compromise.

## 7. Recommendation

**Recommend option (a): scope launch to US/USD and defer the native-currency ledger — conditional
on the research gate below.**

**One-line rationale:** the correctness debt is *zero* for a USD launch audience, option (b) is a
money-layer-wide change with an inherently lossy migration, and whether a non-USD audience is even
in scope is unanswered — so launch USD-only with the currency picker constrained, and build (b)
only when international is a committed goal.

### Research gate (this is what the recommendation is conditional on)

**Is an international (non-USD) audience in scope for launch?**

- **No / US-first (assumed default):** do **(a)**. Zero cost, zero correctness debt for the
  launch audience. Close the in-between: constrain the currency picker to USD (or label
  non-USD as approximate) so no one silently lands on the drifting model.
- **Yes (non-USD households at launch):** **(b) is mandatory**, and must be sequenced **before**
  the rest of the money layer is hardened — it redefines "correct" for every stored amount, so
  hardening on USD cents first would be rework.

There is no third answer that ships a non-USD audience on today's model.

### If/when option (b) is greenlit — sequencing

1. Decide the aggregation policy first (same-currency-only vs. convert-at-display-with-marker).
2. Schema + `currency` enum, then rewrite writes, then reads/aggregates, then regenerate the
   vector harness with per-currency + mixed-currency cases.
3. Accept and document the one-way migration loss for pre-existing rows (`currency = 'usd'`).
